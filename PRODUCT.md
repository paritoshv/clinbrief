## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js + Tailwind (user's explicit choice). Single marketing surface today; chosen for the team's familiarity and room to grow into more pages later.

## Users

**Visitors to this site:** investors and prospective hospital/BD partners. The site is a credibility surface for fundraising and partnership conversations, not an inbound self-serve funnel. Success is a reply to the contact CTA, not a signup.

**Users of the product itself (context, not the site's audience):**
- **Patients** — receive the call before a scheduled consultation. Any age, any literacy level, across India, speaking one of many languages. They did not ask for the call and have no app.
- **Doctors** — consume the structured brief immediately before or during the consultation. Time-poor; high OPD volume.
- **Hospitals** — the buyer. Care about OPD throughput, doctor time, and patient experience.

## Product Purpose

ClinBrief is an AI voice agent that phones patients ahead of a scheduled consultation, conducts the history-taking conversation in the patient's own language, and delivers a structured clinical brief to the doctor before the patient walks in. The consultation starts informed instead of starting from zero.

Success: doctors open the consult already knowing the history; hospitals see shorter, better consultations without adding staff.

## Positioning

Pre-consultation history-taking, conducted by voice, in every major Indian language. The mechanism a neighbouring product cannot easily copy is the combination of (a) the phone call as the interface — no app, no literacy requirement, no patient onboarding — and (b) genuine multilingual coverage across India rather than English-plus-Hindi.

## Operating Context

Hospital OPD scheduling systems produce a list of tomorrow's appointments. ClinBrief calls those patients in the window before their slot. The output lands wherever the doctor already looks before a consult. The call is outbound to a patient who did not initiate it, which makes the opening seconds — identification, hospital attribution, trust — the hardest part of the product.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Pre-launch.** No live pilots, no hospitals onboarded, no usage data.
- **Undecided / must not be claimed on the site:** exact number of languages live; EMR/HIS integrations; ABDM or ABHA participation; DPDP Act compliance posture; pricing; deployment model; clinical validation.
- Terminology: the call produces a *pre-consultation brief*. ClinBrief does not diagnose, triage, or advise — stating otherwise is a regulatory claim.

## Brand Commitments

- Name is **ClinBrief** (renamed from the earlier working name "Asha").
- Standalone brand. **HealthifyMe backing is not referenceable on the site** — do not mention or imply it.
- The agent's voice persona is female, consistently, across all languages.
- Calls always carry hospital attribution ("ClinBrief, calling from [Hospital]") — this is a product commitment, and the site should reflect it rather than positioning ClinBrief as a direct-to-patient service.

## Product Principles

1. **The phone is the accessibility strategy.** No app, no typing, no literacy assumption. Every product and design decision should protect that.
2. **Trust is won in the first eight seconds of a call.** Identification and hospital attribution outrank efficiency.
3. **The output is for a doctor with ninety seconds.** Structure and brevity beat completeness.
4. **Language coverage is the moat, not a feature bullet.** India is not one market and the product should never be described as though it is.
5. **Never imply clinical judgement.** ClinBrief gathers; the doctor decides.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The product's own users span low literacy, low digital fluency, and elderly populations reached by voice. The site's audience is investors and partners, so standard WCAG AA applies to the site itself — but the site should demonstrate awareness that the product's inclusion story is its core thesis.
